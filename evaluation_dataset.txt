id: a13_emergency
category: emergency
mission: apollo_13
question: What emergency did the Apollo 13 crew report, and what was the cause?
expected_answer: The crew heard a pretty large bang with a caution and warning and reported "Houston, we've had a problem" together with a Main B bus undervolt. They then saw that they were venting something into space. Mission control described it as an apparent serious oxygen leak in the cryogenic oxygen in the service module, which took fuel cells off the line and forced the crew to power down the command module.
evidence: apollo13/AS13_PAO, apollo13/AS13_TEC (air-to-ground transcript and Apollo Control commentary)

id: a13_technical
category: technical
mission: apollo_13
question: How did the Apollo 13 crew deal with carbon dioxide removal while using the lunar module?
expected_answer: The crew used the lunar module Aquarius as a lifeboat, so mission control worked out a procedure to use the command module's lithium hydroxide cartridges for CO2 removal in the lunar module. The crew built a CO2 "rig" by modifying a cartridge with plastic bags or covers, grey tape, and something like a sock to plug the bottom so that the air flowed through the cartridge.
evidence: apollo13/AS13_PAO (lifeboat commentary and CO2 cartridge procedure)

question: What happened to Challenger during the ascent of mission STS-51L?
expected_answer: About a minute into the flight, shortly after the three main engines throttled back up to 104 percent and the call "Challenger, go at throttle up", the public affairs officer said "Obviously a major malfunction". There was no downlink, and the flight dynamics officer reported that the vehicle had exploded. Contingency procedures were put into effect, and tracking reported the impact of the vehicle with the water about 18 miles downrange.
